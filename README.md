### Little Red Riding Hood

Little Red Riding Hood is a 2D platformer telling the story of the classic fairytale (with a slight change at the end).

Download and play the game: https://sophia990.itch.io/little-red-riding-hood

**Technical Details**

Engine: Unity 2022.3.14f1 (LTS)

Template: Universal 2D + URP 14.0.9

Platforms: Browser

**Controls**

Move - WASD or arrow keys

Jump - Space

Sword attack - Right shift

Bow attack - F

**Project Description**

I acquired various new skills over the course of creating this game, such as using Unity Events to cause code to run in one script when something happens in a different script, and coroutines, for time delays in particular.

This was also my first Unity project in 2D, which came with many new opportunities to learn. Something I used for the first time was tilemaps, to draw the ground for the player.

However, by far the largest challenge was creating cutscenes. I used Unity timeline to animate characters, and trigger dialogue and scene changes. 

In this project, I wanted there to be a few different types of enemies, as well as different ways to interact with the map and environments. I also, however, did not want to overwhelm the player from the beginning, so I made the choice to introduce some enemies - the bats and flowers that attack - in later levels only. Additionally, I kept the map relatively simple in the first levels, then included more complex environmental features in the later levels.

In this version of the story, Little Red Riding Hood doesn’t need a lumberjack to save her and her grandmother, but defeats the wolf and saves her grandmother by herself. In fact, in a revised narrative still in development, Little Red Riding Hood also saves the lumberjack that has been eaten by the wolf earlier.

My decision to modify the ending of the fairytale is rooted in the frustration I experienced growing up with so many stories of damsels in distress being saved by a man, including the original Little Red Riding Hood. Therefore, when I chose this story as one that I would make into a game, I wanted to channel this frustration into something creative and write a new narrative.

The game is targeted towards children aged seven and up, or younger children with parents. It is intentionally a short game, around 20-30 minutes long, to give the player a sense of achievement when they finish it, and to make it ideal for parents wanting to introduce games to their child in a positive fashion.
